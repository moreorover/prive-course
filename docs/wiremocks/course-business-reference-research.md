# Small Course Business Reference Research

## Purpose

Product Atelier should not be benchmarked only against SaaS dashboards or generic course catalogs. The closer reference set is small creator-led education businesses that sell a focused set of video courses, often with a founder, a practical outcome, a course ladder, and an email capture path.

This research should inform the next Excalidraw pass before more Mantine implementation work.

## Product Atelier Context

Product Atelier will host three courses:

- Hair Extensions Course - Basic
- Hair Extensions Course - Basic + Pro
- Social Media Marketing Strategy

Access model:

- Basic gives access to Basic.
- Basic + Pro gives access to Basic and Basic + Pro.
- Social Media Marketing Strategy gives access to Social Media Marketing Strategy.

No checkout or automated purchase logic is required for v1. The site still needs to explain the offer clearly, collect update subscribers, and make private video access feel intentional.

## Reference Sites

### Dreams of Code

URL: https://dreamsofcode.io/courses

Category: independent developer education.

Observed structure:

- Simple courses page with a small catalog.
- Course cards show status such as available or coming soon.
- Each course has a compact topic label and direct action.
- The page closes with a simple “ready to level up” conversion prompt.

Useful for Product Atelier:

- Small inventory does not need a complex marketplace UI.
- Course availability and access state can be explicit.
- A small course catalog can feel credible if hierarchy is clean.

Avoid copying:

- Developer-course minimalism may be too sparse for beauty buyers.
- Product Atelier needs more creator trust, practical outcomes, and visual warmth than a technical course catalog.

### Beauty Lab GC - Complete Extension Masterclass

URL: https://www.beautylabgc.com/the-complete-extension-masterclass

Category: hair extension education by a salon educator.

Observed structure:

- Direct course promise: learn multiple extension methods online and self-paced.
- Strong “what’s inside” breakdown split between video tutorials and theory modules.
- Outcome section explains why the course pays for itself.
- Audience section names beginner stylists, experienced hairdressers, and salon owners.
- Instructor section builds trust with a founder story and real portrait.
- Final price and enrol CTA are repeated near the bottom.
- FAQ resolves practical buying concerns.
- Subscribe prompt appears in the footer area.

Useful for Product Atelier:

- Course pages should sell “why this matters” before showing lesson lists.
- Beauty buyers need method coverage, client/business outcomes, and instructor credibility.
- The Basic + Pro course can be framed around higher-value client services and repeat bookings.

Avoid copying:

- Product Atelier should not add fake income claims.
- If numbers are used, they need to come from real business data or be removed.

### Bela Beauty College - Hair Extension Course

URL: https://belabeautycollege.com/products/hair-extension-course

Category: online beauty course commerce.

Observed structure:

- Product page has price, kit selection, payment options, and enrol actions near the top.
- Course value is supported by certificate, access duration, student community, kit shipping, mentor support, and reviews.
- Course details are concrete: duration, format, kit, certificate, support.
- Modules are listed with numbers and specific topics.
- Kit section explains tangible materials.
- FAQ is broad and practical.
- Reviews and community section strengthen confidence.
- Bundle upsell appears after the main course content.

Useful for Product Atelier:

- Course detail pages need stronger content density than a generic lesson list.
- Modules should look like a curriculum, not just links.
- Access benefits can be expressed in plain buyer language: private lessons, previews, granted access, updates.
- Basic + Pro can be visually treated as a bundle without adding purchase logic.

Avoid copying:

- Product Atelier does not need ecommerce controls, payment-plan blocks, kit selection, or cart UI in v1.
- Heavy commerce density would overpromise if payment is out of scope.

### LA Lash Academy

URL: https://www.lalashacademy.com/

Category: beauty training school with online and in-person paths.

Observed structure:

- Brand-forward hero with strong founder/school identity.
- Two clear learning paths: online class and in-person intensive.
- Online course offer includes price, pro kit, lifetime access, and start-now language.
- Founder section turns teaching history into credibility.
- Curriculum is shown as chapters and lesson counts.
- FAQ and final “choose your training path” section help users self-select.

Useful for Product Atelier:

- A course business can feel premium and structured without looking like SaaS.
- Path selection can be a primary page concept.
- Founder credibility and teaching origin matter in beauty education.
- Course pages can show chapter-like structure instead of a flat lesson list.

Avoid copying:

- Their visual language uses many decorative labels and dense editorial styling. Product Atelier should be cleaner and less noisy.
- Product Atelier should avoid theatrical counters or visual metadata unless backed by real data.

## Cross-Site Patterns

### Patterns To Borrow

- Small catalogs work best when the offers are positioned as paths, not equal cards.
- Beauty education pages need instructor credibility and real method coverage.
- Course detail pages should show outcomes before curriculum.
- Curriculum should be chapter-like, with visible progression.
- Access terms should be plain: self-paced, lifetime or time-limited access, private lessons, updates, support.
- Bundle relationships should be visible without requiring payment logic.
- Email capture works better when attached to a reason: course updates, launch list, free resource, or private student updates.
- FAQs matter because course buyers need to resolve practical doubts before paying or signing in.
- Course status states are useful: available, coming soon, free preview, protected, included, locked.

### Patterns To Avoid

- Three identical course cards as the main homepage structure.
- Generic SaaS dashboard styling for a beauty education business.
- Ecommerce controls when payments are out of scope.
- Fake statistics, fake income claims, fake student counts, or fake urgency.
- Overly dense commerce layouts that imply checkout exists.
- Placeholder benefit labels.
- Decorative technical metadata that does not help a beauty course buyer.
- All-caps sales tone unless the brand intentionally wants that.

## Implications For The Next Wiremock

### Home

The homepage should behave like a guided product ladder:

- Hero introduces the platform and three-course system.
- Hair extension path is shown as Basic to Basic + Pro.
- Social Media Marketing Strategy is shown as the business-growth path.
- Subscribe form is a major conversion band.
- Founder/teaching credibility should appear if we have real content or can reserve a slot for it.

### Courses Catalog

The catalog should be a guided selection screen:

- Feature Basic + Pro as the expanded path.
- Show Basic as the starting point.
- Show Social Media Marketing Strategy as the separate growth course.
- Use status/access states clearly.
- Avoid a generic equal card grid.

### Course Detail

Each course detail page should feel like a course product page:

- Course promise.
- Best-for audience.
- What is inside.
- Chapter/curriculum path.
- Access state and next action.
- FAQ or practical details if content exists.

### Lesson Page

The lesson page should feel like private learning software:

- Player first.
- Lesson queue as a workspace control.
- Progress/status visible.
- Notes secondary.
- Locked state framed as an access decision, not a generic error.

### Admin And Profile

Admin and profile should not borrow marketing layouts:

- Profile is a settings hub.
- Admin is a course operations surface.
- Use denser product UI, clearer workflows, and fewer promotional visual devices.

## Recommended Design Direction

Use a hybrid of:

- Beauty Lab GC for course value and instructor credibility.
- Bela Beauty College for concrete module and access detail.
- LA Lash Academy for path selection and curriculum framing.
- Rachael Boer for creator-led ecosystem and lead capture.
- Dreams of Code for small catalog simplicity and course status clarity.

The next Excalidraw pass should move away from “homepage plus cards” and toward a site model with three explicit jobs:

1. Help a visitor choose the right course path.
2. Make each course feel like a substantial product.
3. Make the learning area feel private, focused, and intentional.
